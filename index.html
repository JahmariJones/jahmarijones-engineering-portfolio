Electromagnetic Generator

A motor-driven generator built to light an LED. A spinning magnet core induces voltage in an 800-turn copper coil, and the output powers an LED through a hand-soldered PCB.

  • Compared design concepts with a weighted Pugh matrix and combined a magnet-and-coil core with gear-driven speed multiplication.
  • Swapped the planned belt drive for a 20:10 spur gear pair, doubling rotor speed without slip.
  • Oriented the magnets perpendicular to the coil to maximize flux, following Faraday's law.
  • Soldered the PCB that powers the LED.
  • Traced LED flicker on the scope to magnet shift, gear friction, and vibration, and documented fixes for a next revision.

Model Wind Turbine

Scaled wind tunnel model of a recreational marine wind turbine: a three-blade, straight-blade H-rotor vertical-axis wind turbine (VAWT) coupled to an RS-340 permanent-magnet DC motor operating as the generator. The model was used to characterize how airfoil blade profile affects cut-in wind speed and generated voltage.

Small-scale model: low output by design. At this rotor diameter and swept area, and with a small brushed DC generator, the measured terminal voltage is low and peak electrical power stays below 20 mW. Results are intended for relative comparison between blade profiles, not for usable power generation.

  • Designed the complete CAD model in Fusion 360, including all four blade profiles, and manufactured all components.
  • Rotor: three 3D-printed airfoil blades on acrylic support arms, a central shaft coupled directly to the generator, and a base plate that mounts the motor in the wind tunnel test section.
  • Test matrix: four blade profiles (flat plate, NACA 0015, NACA 2418, NACA 4415), each run across wind speeds of roughly 3.4–11.7 m/s while recording generator output voltage and calculating electrical power.
  • Cut-in: all three cambered and symmetric airfoils self-started between 3.38 and 3.66 m/s, 27–33% lower than the flat plate (5.04 m/s). None met the 3.0 m/s design target.
  • Power: NACA 2418 delivered the highest output across approximately 6–10 m/s, the range of typical open-water wind speeds, and is the recommended profile for the full-scale design. NACA 4415 recorded the single highest reading, about 18.7 mW near 11 m/s.
